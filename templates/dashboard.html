How to read this. Detection asks whether a number is unusual against its own trailing history, so it fires on dozens of segments at once: a broken slice poisons every container that holds it. Attribution weights each slice by the traffic it covers (contribution = share × delta), which separates a cause from a passenger. The exclusion test then removes one candidate at a time and recomputes the global figure. If the anomaly disappears for exactly one slice and persists for every other, nothing else contributed. That asymmetry is the proof.

Known gaps. Revenue at risk is a counterfactual against baseline, not a booked loss. Composite metrics are not suppressed, so one root cause can open more than one incident (eCPM and revenue-per-request on the same window are the same event).
